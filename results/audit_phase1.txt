A1/A2 [FIXED] The score-SDE sampler did not sample from the data distribution
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILES: src/its/sde/score_sde.py:66 ; src/its/training/controlled_score_training.py:531 (duplicate)

WAS:  drift = 0.5*beta_t*(-x - score)          # = -0.5*beta*x - 0.5*beta*score
      x = x + drift*step_size + sqrt(beta_t*step_size)*noise   # step_size=+1/N while t DESCENDS

Three compounding errors:
  (i)   sign inverted on BOTH drift terms (positive step used with a reverse-drift formula)
  (ii)  score coefficient 0.5*beta instead of beta (factor 2)
  (iii) the model is trained with a VE kernel (score_training.py:244 `noisy = x + sigma*noise`,
        target `-noise/sigma`, log-uniform sigma in [0.01,1]) but sampled with VP linear-beta
        dynamics. There is no SDE for which this (sigma-schedule, beta-schedule) pair is a
        discretisation.

PROOF (executed, exact analytic score, data N(0, 0.5^2), target variance 0.25):
    steps      code's rule      corrected rule
      50       var = 20.32      var = 0.2570
     100       var = 20.65      var = 0.2525
     200       var = 21.00      var = 0.2511
     500       var = 21.13      var = 0.2516
  The code's rule converges to ~84x the true variance and does NOT improve with NFE.
  This exactly predicts the observed SDE baseline FID 326.09/325.20/325.69 at NFE
  50/100/200 -- flat, which is impossible for a working sampler.
  Independently reproduced by a reader agent on N(3.0,0.5^2): the loop returns
  mean ~= -9.87 (target +3.0), and with the score forced to zero returns N(0,I),
  i.e. it relaxes to noise regardless of the score model.

FIX: implemented the correct VE reverse-diffusion predictor matching the VE-trained
     model: x <- x + dvar*score + sqrt(dvar)*z with dvar = sigma_i^2 - sigma_{i-1}^2.
     Added ScoreSDEConfig.parameterisation ("ve" default | "legacy_vp"), the latter
     retained ONLY to reproduce archived runs and documented as never valid for a
     result. VE prior init corrected to N(0, sigma_max^2) (was always N(0,I)).
     Applied to BOTH the eval sampler and the training rollout (simulate_path).

VERIFIED AFTER FIX (executed, analytic score, target mean 3.0 / std 0.5):
    NFE=50  -> mean +2.9654 std 0.5328
    NFE=100 -> mean +2.9696 std 0.5149
    NFE=200 -> mean +2.9688 std 0.5085
    NFE=500 -> mean +2.9703 std 0.5046      <-- converges AND improves with NFE
    legacy_vp for contrast -> mean -74.30 std 20.65 (and worsening with NFE)

A4 [FIXED] The Girsanov log-Radon-Nikodym / path-KL formula was wrong
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILES: controlled_score_training.py:544,549 ; sde/score_sde.py:83,109 ;
       objectives/loss_components.py:57,238  (FOUR copies of the same wrong expression)

WAS:  log_rn_step = <u,eps>*sqrt(beta*dt) - 0.5*||u||^2*dt   ;  path_kl = -log_rn.mean()

TRUE: log_rn_step = <delta,z>/sqrt(var) + ||delta||^2/(2*var)  ;  path_kl = +log_rn.mean()
      (delta = the drift shift actually integrated; var = the step's noise variance)

PROOF (executed against exact Gaussian log-densities):
      correct formula vs ground truth: err = 3.3e-16
      code's formula  vs ground truth: err = 9.4e-01 in a SINGLE step
      code's path_kl off by exactly a factor beta_t (= 3.0 in the test)
      Independently Monte-Carlo verified by an adversarial agent to 4.4e-15.
  Since beta sweeps 0.1 -> 10 during sampling this is a TIME-VARYING distortion; it
  cannot be absorbed into path_kl_weight. Path-space KL is one of the five headline
  quantities the framework claims to track: every path-KL ever reported is wrong.

  *** CORRECTION FROM ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION (recorded because it matters) ***
  My initial claim said the code "multiplies by beta in both terms". That is WRONG:
  the quadratic term contains NO beta at all -- it OMITS the required 1/beta. The
  net error is a factor of beta in both terms, but by a different mechanism.
  More importantly, a verifier proved that fixing line 544 ALONE would be
  CATASTROPHIC: line 549's `path_kl = -log_rn.mean()` sign-compensates the error,
  and correcting the formula without flipping that sign yields a "path KL" that is
  NEGATIVE and unbounded below in ||u||. The two MUST change together. This is why
  the bug survived ten sessions: two sign errors cancelled, producing a positive,
  plausible-looking ~0.5*||u||^2*dt.

FIX: created src/its/sde/girsanov.py holding the SINGLE correct implementation,
     stated in terms of the realised drift shift and realised noise variance (so it
     is correct for VP, VE, or any Euler scheme). All four call-sites now use it.
     The sign flip and the formula were changed together, and both functions carry
     a comment stating they are a matched pair.

B2 [FIXED] The v2 REINFORCE objective was unbounded below (caused the 5A divergence)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILE: src/its/objectives/loss_components.py:159

WAS:  baseline = rewards.detach().mean()
      return -((rewards.detach() - baseline) * log_probs).mean()

  log_probs (the Girsanov exponent) grows QUADRATICALLY in ||u|| and is unbounded.
  With a mean-zero baseline, ~half of every batch has advantage < 0 BY CONSTRUCTION,
  and for those samples -A_i*log_prob_i -> -inf as ||u|| -> inf. Additionally the
  advantage was not standardised and rewards were not in [0,1] as the docstring
  claimed -- they reached ~1e6.

  WHY grad_clip=1.0 DID NOT SAVE IT: clipping bounds step SIZE, not divergence. On
  an objective unbounded below, clipped descent still marches to infinity at a
  bounded rate. The observed STEADY MONOTONIC growth of the control/score ratio
  (19.9 -> 55,000 over 30 epochs) is exactly that signature.

  *** CORRECTION FROM ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION ***
  The divergence has TWO phases, not one (my initial single-cause account was wrong):
    Phase 1 (ep 1-5): WarmupSchedule zeroes path_kl_weight AND reinforce_weight while
      detach_control_energy=True removes control energy from the gradient -> ||u||
      has NO REGULARISER AT ALL. Control energy grew 25,000x (0.21 -> 5301) before
      any Girsanov term was switched on. Ratio already 19.9 at epoch 1.
    Phase 2 (ep 6+): reinforce_weight becomes non-zero and total_loss goes negative
      at EXACTLY epoch 6 -> the unbounded surrogate takes over -> -1.59e14.
  Also proven: fixing the Girsanov sign alone does NOT fix this. With the corrected
  log_rn ~ +||u||^2/(2*var), the pathology MIGRATES to positive-advantage samples.
  The surrogate itself must be bounded.

FIX: standardise the advantage by its std (scale-free, standard variance reduction),
     clamp the standardised advantage to +/-5, clamp the log-prob factor to +/-50.
     Both clips apply to the score-function COEFFICIENT, so the estimator remains an
     unbiased policy gradient in the unclipped region.

C1 [FIXED] The trajectory quality loss had a silently broken gradient
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILE: src/its/objectives/loss_components.py:139

WAS:  gen_sq = (gen_feats**2).sum(1, keepdim=True)
      with torch.no_grad():
          real_sq = ...
          cross = gen_feats @ real_feats.T      # <-- INSIDE no_grad: DETACHED
      dist2 = gen_sq + real_sq.T - 2*cross      # gradient flows ONLY through gen_sq

PROOF (executed): the forward value is BIT-IDENTICAL to a correct cdist
  implementation, but the gradient is exactly 2*gen_feats/(B*tau) -- the gradient of
  ||phi(gen)||^2, i.e. pure shrinkage of Inception features toward the ORIGIN, with
  ZERO pull toward the nearest real sample. Cosine similarity to the correct
  gradient: 0.7415. Confirmed bit-for-bit by three independent verifiers; one noted
  the nearest-neighbour search is 100% gradient-irrelevant (all columns share the
  same gradient).
  This was the load-bearing fix Session 10 introduced to CURE the collapse, and it
  did not do what it claimed.

FIX: moved `cross` out of the no_grad block (only real_sq, which depends solely on
     already-detached real_feats, remains detached).

PART 7 — VERDICT
================================================================================
IS THE CODEBASE READY FOR THE PHASE 2 TRAINING RUNS?

  NO — and that is the honest answer, not a hedge. Two blockers remain, neither of
  which is a code defect:

  1. PHASE 0 (environment): torch is CPU-only. Training is ~50-100x too slow to be
     viable. Requires a confirmed reinstall.

  2. GATE 2.2 (substrate validation): the sampler is now provably correct ON AN
     ANALYTIC SCORE. It has NOT been shown correct on the real score model, because
     that requires generating samples -- an experimental result, which this phase is
     forbidden from producing until the code is correct. The FIRST action of Phase 2
     must be to retrain the score model under the now-consistent kernel and verify
     the UNCONTROLLED sampler reaches FID < 30 at NFE=100 AND improves with NFE.
     Until that gate passes, no ITS number means anything.

WHAT PHASE 1 DID ACHIEVE:
  - The four fatal defects (A1, A2, A3, A4) are fixed, each verified by execution.
  - The v2 objective's unboundedness (B2) and its broken quality gradient (C1) are
    fixed -- these are why run 5A diverged and why the Session 10 redesign could
    never have worked.
  - The Girsanov expression is de-duplicated from four copies into one tested module.
  - The test suite can now FAIL on broken science; every new test was falsified
    against the pre-audit code to prove it.
  - Three open defects and two blocked actions are documented rather than hidden.

WHAT PHASE 1 CANNOT ACHIEVE:
  No amount of code correctness recovers the existing results. Every experimental
  number in this project is void and must be regenerated from a retrained score
  model. The project is further from a paper than its own record suggested. See
  docs/roadmap_current.md.
